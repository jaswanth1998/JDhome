When people start shopping for security cameras, the first big choice is between **wired PoE cameras** and **wireless Wi-Fi or battery cameras**. Both have their place. For most permanent home and business installs, though, PoE wins on reliability. Here's why, and when wireless still makes sense.

## What is PoE?

PoE stands for **Power over Ethernet**. A single network cable (Cat5e or Cat6) runs from each camera back to a PoE switch or a network video recorder (NVR) with built-in PoE ports. That one cable carries both:

- **Power** to run the camera, its night-vision lights, and heaters on some outdoor models
- **Data**, meaning the video stream back to the recorder

There's no need for an electrical outlet at the camera location.

## What are Wi-Fi and battery cameras?

- **Wi-Fi cameras** send video over your wireless network but still need power, usually from a nearby outlet.
- **Battery cameras** are fully wireless but need recharging and typically record only when motion is detected, to save battery.

## Head-to-head comparison

### Reliability

- **PoE:** a wired connection doesn't drop out because of interference, a busy network, or a weak signal through brick walls.
- **Wi-Fi:** reliability depends on router placement, signal strength, and how many devices share the network. Outdoor cameras on the far side of a brick house are often at the edge of coverage.

### Video quality

- **PoE:** easily carries high-resolution, continuous video from many cameras at once.
- **Wi-Fi:** high-resolution streams from several cameras can strain home networks, so video may be compressed or choppy.

### Continuous recording

- **PoE:** records 24/7 to an NVR if you want it, so you never miss the moment before motion was detected.
- **Battery:** usually records clips only when triggered, which can miss the start of an event.

### Winter performance

Cold Ontario winters shorten battery life significantly, so battery cameras may need charging more often in January than in July. PoE cameras are powered continuously and aren't affected by battery drain.

### Security

A wired network is harder to interfere with than a wireless one. Some wireless cameras can be affected by signal jamming or network outages.

### Installation

- **PoE:** requires running cable, which takes planning and professional work to conceal neatly.
- **Wi-Fi/battery:** quick to put up, which makes them handy for renters or temporary needs.

### Long-term cost

PoE systems cost more to install because of the cabling, but there are no batteries to replace and, with a local NVR, no required cloud subscription. Wireless cameras often rely on per-camera cloud plans for recording.

## Useful PoE facts

- A standard Ethernet run can be up to 100 metres (about 328 feet) from the switch or NVR to the camera, which covers almost any home or small business.
- PoE standards deliver power levels suited to typical cameras; higher-power PTZ cameras may need PoE+ ports.
- One NVR with built-in PoE ports can power and record a whole system from one box.

## When wireless still makes sense

- A single camera somewhere cabling is impractical, like a far fence post
- Renters who can't run cables
- Temporary monitoring, such as during renovations

A hybrid system is fine: PoE for the main coverage, and one wireless camera for a hard-to-reach spot.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do PoE cameras need internet?

No. They record to the NVR without internet. Internet is only needed for remote viewing and phone alerts.

### Can PoE cameras work in a power outage?

If the NVR or PoE switch is connected to a battery backup (UPS), the whole system keeps running for a while during an outage.

### Is Cat6 better than Cat5e for cameras?

Both work well for typical camera systems. Cat6 offers more headroom for future upgrades.

### Can you run PoE cable to a detached garage?

Often, yes, with an appropriate outdoor-rated or buried cable route. We assess this during the site visit.

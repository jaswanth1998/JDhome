Every camera system has to answer one question: **where does the video go?** There are two main options, a network video recorder (NVR) at your property or cloud storage on someone else's servers. Each has strengths, and many people end up with a mix.

## What is an NVR?

An NVR is a small box, usually installed in a closet, basement, or utility room, with one or more hard drives inside. With PoE cameras, the NVR often powers the cameras too. It:

- Records video from every camera, continuously or on events
- Runs or stores AI detection results on many systems
- Lets you watch and search footage on a monitor or through a phone app

## What is cloud storage?

With cloud recording, each camera uploads video over your internet connection to the manufacturer's servers. You view clips through their app. Most cloud plans charge a monthly fee per camera or per household and keep footage for a set number of days.

## Comparison

### Privacy and control

- **NVR:** footage stays on your property. You decide who has access and how long it's kept.
- **Cloud:** footage lives on a third party's servers under their policies.

### Ongoing cost

- **NVR:** a one-time purchase of the recorder and drives. No required monthly fee.
- **Cloud:** ongoing subscription fees that add up over the years, especially with several cameras.

### Recording quality and continuity

- **NVR:** can record high-resolution video 24/7 from every camera, because it isn't limited by your upload speed.
- **Cloud:** continuous high-resolution upload from many cameras uses a lot of bandwidth, so cloud plans often record only event clips.

### Reliability when the internet is down

- **NVR:** keeps recording without internet.
- **Cloud:** if the internet drops, recording may stop or rely on a small local buffer.

### Theft or damage risk

- **NVR:** if someone finds and takes the recorder, footage goes with it. Mitigate by installing it out of sight, in a locked cabinet or closet.
- **Cloud:** footage is stored off-site, so it survives if equipment is stolen.

## How long does an NVR keep footage?

It depends on drive size, number of cameras, resolution, and whether recording is continuous or event-based. Modern compression formats (such as H.265) stretch storage significantly. When the drive fills up, the NVR overwrites the oldest footage automatically. We size the storage so you keep a sensible number of days for your needs.

## The hybrid approach

Many of our customers choose:

- **Local NVR** for continuous, high-quality recording and smart search, with no monthly fees
- **Remote phone access** for live view, playback, and AI alerts from anywhere
- **Exporting important clips** to your phone or cloud storage when you need to share them

If off-site backup matters, some systems can also send selected event clips to cloud storage.

## Tips for protecting your NVR

- Install it somewhere discreet, not beside the front door
- Connect it to a small battery backup (UPS) to ride through short power outages
- Use strong, unique passwords and keep firmware updated
- Change default admin credentials during setup

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a monitor for an NVR?

No. Most people use the phone app. A monitor is handy for businesses that want a live view on a screen.

### Can I watch NVR footage away from home?

Yes, once the NVR is connected to your internet and set up in the app.

### Is an NVR the same as a DVR?

Not quite. DVRs typically work with older analogue cameras over coaxial cable; NVRs work with IP cameras over a network, usually PoE.

### What happens when the NVR's hard drive is full?

It overwrites the oldest recordings first, so it keeps recording continuously.

A garage door will never be silent, but it shouldn't wake the house either. If yours has gone from a gentle hum to a racket, the noise is telling you something. Some sounds are harmless and easy to fix. Others are early warnings of a part about to fail.

Here's how to decode the most common garage door noises.

## Squeaking or squealing

**Likely cause:** dry hinges, roller bearings, or springs.

Metal-on-metal parts need occasional lubrication, especially after a dry summer or a cold snap. A light spray of garage-door lubricant on the hinges, roller bearings, and springs often quiets things immediately. Avoid heavy grease, and don't spray the tracks.

## Grinding or scraping

**Likely causes:** worn rollers, a bent track, or a door rubbing against the frame.

Old steel rollers wear flat and grind as they turn. Cracked nylon rollers wobble and scrape. A door that's slightly out of alignment can rub against the tracks or the weatherstripping. Replacing worn rollers with quality sealed-bearing nylon rollers is one of the most effective ways to quiet a door, but roller replacement near the bottom brackets involves spring tension and should be done by a technician.

## Rattling or vibrating

**Likely cause:** loose nuts and bolts.

A garage door moves thousands of times a year, and vibration loosens hardware. Tightening hinge and track bolts with a socket wrench can help. **Don't loosen or adjust the bottom brackets**; they're connected to the lift cables and are under tension.

## Banging or popping

**Likely causes:** a spring problem, a cable issue, or a door section catching.

A single loud bang, especially followed by a door that won't lift, usually means a [broken spring](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring/). Repeated popping or snapping as the door moves can mean a spring is binding, a roller is jumping in the track, or a hinge is cracked. These need attention quickly.

## Humming or buzzing without movement

**Likely causes:** the opener is straining against a door it can't lift, or the motor or gears are failing.

Unplug the opener and check whether the door is disconnected, locked, frozen to the floor, or held by a broken spring. Don't keep pressing the button. See our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/garage-door-wont-open/).

## Chain slapping or clanking

**Likely cause:** a loose chain on a chain-drive opener.

Chain drives are durable but naturally louder. A chain that sags too much will slap the rail. Adjusting the tension (per the manufacturer's instructions) helps. If noise matters because there's a bedroom above the garage, a belt-drive or wall-mount opener is much quieter. Our [opener buying guide](/blog/choosing-a-garage-door-opener/) compares the types.

## Quick fixes you can safely try

1. **Lubricate** hinges, roller bearings, and springs with a garage-door lubricant.
2. **Tighten** hinge and track bolts (not bottom brackets).
3. **Clean the tracks** with a damp cloth. Don't lubricate them.
4. **Check the opener chain** tension using your manual.
5. **Watch the door move** to see whether it's even on both sides.

## When noise means it's time to call

Call a technician if:

- You hear a loud bang and the door becomes heavy
- The door jerks, shudders, or moves unevenly
- Rollers are popping out of the track
- Cables look frayed or loose
- Noise continues after lubrication and tightening

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the quietest type of garage door opener?

Belt-drive and wall-mount (jackshaft) openers are generally the quietest. Chain drives are the loudest, though modern models with DC motors are quieter than older ones.

### Do nylon rollers really make a difference?

Yes. Quality nylon rollers with sealed bearings run much more quietly than worn steel rollers and need less maintenance.

### Can I use WD-40 on my garage door?

Standard WD-40 is mainly a cleaner and water displacer, not a long-lasting lubricant. Use a silicone or white lithium product labelled for garage doors.

### Is a noisy garage door dangerous?

Not always, but noise is often the first sign of wear. Grinding, banging, and jerky movement are worth checking before a part fails.

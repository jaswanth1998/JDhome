If you've ever owned a basic motion-detection camera, you know the problem: your phone buzzes all day for swaying branches, passing headlights, rain, snow, shadows, and the neighbour's cat. After a week, most people turn notifications off entirely, which defeats the point.

**AI person and vehicle detection** fixes that. Here's how it works and how to get the most from it.

## Motion detection vs AI detection

**Traditional motion detection** compares video frames and triggers when enough pixels change. It can't tell *what* changed. A tree moving in the wind looks the same to it as a person walking up the driveway.

**AI detection** runs a trained model, either on the camera itself or on the recorder, that recognizes shapes. It classifies what it sees, typically as:

- **Person**
- **Vehicle** (cars, trucks, sometimes bikes)
- On some systems, **animals** or packages

You then choose which types trigger recordings and alerts.

## Why it matters

- **Far fewer false alerts**, so you actually pay attention to notifications
- **Faster review**, because recordings are tagged by what was detected
- **Smarter search**, letting you pull up "all people at the back door last night" in seconds (see [searching footage by description](/blog/search-camera-footage-by-description/))
- **Better use of storage**, since event recordings are more meaningful

## Setting it up well

### Detection zones

Draw zones on the camera view where alerts should trigger, such as your driveway or walkway, and exclude the public sidewalk or street. This cuts alerts from passers-by and respects neighbours' privacy.

### Line crossing and intrusion

Many systems let you draw a virtual line (for example across a gate) or an area (a backyard). The camera alerts only when a person crosses the line or enters the area, not when they walk past.

### Schedules

Set person alerts to be active overnight or while you're away, and quieter during the day when family members are coming and going.

### Sensitivity

Start at a medium setting and adjust over a few days.

## Camera placement affects AI accuracy

AI needs a clear view to recognize people and vehicles:

- Mount cameras roughly two to three metres high, angled down but not straight down
- Avoid pointing directly into bright lights or the rising or setting sun
- Make sure the area is lit at night or the camera has good night vision
- Keep people at a reasonable size in frame; tiny figures at the edge of a wide view are harder to classify

## Limits to be aware of

AI detection is very good, but not perfect:

- Heavy snow, rain, or fog can reduce accuracy
- A person partly hidden behind a car or bush may be missed or detected late
- Very distant subjects may not be classified
- Spider webs across the lens can trigger events at night

That's why we still recommend **continuous recording** to a local NVR alongside AI events. If an alert is missed, the footage is still there. See [NVR vs cloud storage](/blog/nvr-vs-cloud-camera-storage/).

## Privacy considerations

AI detection identifies *types* of objects, like people and vehicles. Some systems offer facial recognition; if you consider it, think carefully about privacy and applicable law, especially for businesses. For most homes, person and vehicle detection is all you need.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does AI detection work at night?

Yes, when the camera has adequate night vision or the area is lit. Colour night-vision cameras with a small light can improve accuracy.

### Will animals trigger person alerts?

Good AI systems are designed to tell people from animals, so pets and wildlife trigger far fewer person alerts than basic motion detection.

### Do I need a subscription for AI detection?

Not necessarily. Many camera systems run AI on the camera or NVR locally, without a cloud subscription.

### Can AI detection tell me when a car enters my driveway?

Yes. Vehicle detection combined with a detection zone or line crossing on your driveway can alert you when a car pulls in.

For a small business, a camera system does more than deter theft. It helps you resolve disputes, check on the shop after hours, review incidents for insurance, and keep staff safer. But a system that isn't planned well can leave blind spots exactly where problems happen.

Here's how we approach camera systems for stores, offices, restaurants, and small warehouses across Durham Region.

## Start with your risks

Every business is different. Consider:

- **Retail:** shoplifting, till discrepancies, after-hours break-ins
- **Restaurants:** back-door deliveries, cash handling, slip-and-fall claims
- **Offices:** entry control, visitors, equipment theft
- **Warehouses and shops:** loading doors, inventory, vehicles in the lot

Write down the three or four situations you most want to capture. That list drives the whole design.

## Where to put cameras

A typical small business system covers:

- **Entrances and exits:** a clear face-level view of everyone coming in, ideally from the inside looking toward the door
- **Point of sale:** the till and counter, framed to show transactions
- **Back door and loading area:** where deliveries and staff come and go
- **Stock rooms and high-value displays**
- **Parking lot:** vehicle coming and going, with vehicle detection
- **Hallways and aisles** that connect these areas

## Privacy basics in Ontario

Businesses that collect personal information, including video of customers and staff, have privacy responsibilities. This isn't legal advice, but good practice generally includes:

- **Posting clear signage** at entrances that video surveillance is in use
- **Not recording in private areas** such as washrooms and change rooms
- **Being careful with audio recording**, which raises additional privacy concerns
- **Limiting who can access footage** and keeping it secure
- **Keeping footage only as long as you need it**, and having a simple written policy
- **Considering staff notice** where cameras cover work areas

The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada publishes guidance on video surveillance by businesses. If you have specific questions, speak with a legal adviser.

## Protecting the system itself

- Install the NVR in a locked office or cabinet
- Put the recorder and network equipment on a battery backup (UPS)
- Use strong passwords, separate user accounts, and keep firmware updated
- Mount outdoor cameras high enough to discourage tampering

## Frequently asked questions

### How many cameras does a small store need?

Many small shops are well covered with four to eight cameras, but it depends on the layout, entrances, and what you want to capture.

### Do I have to post signs that cameras are in use?

Clear signage is widely recommended good practice for businesses using video surveillance in Canada. Check current guidance and get legal advice for your situation.

### Can I check my store cameras from home?

Yes. With the phone app, you can view live video, play back recordings, and receive after-hours alerts.

Most break-ins aren't sophisticated. They happen through an unlocked door, a weak lock, an open garage, or a dark side of the house where nobody is watching. The good news is that a few practical upgrades and habits make a home much less attractive to an opportunist.

Use this checklist to walk around your home.

## Front and back doors

- **Deadbolts on every exterior door**, used every time
- **Solid-core or metal doors** on entrances; hollow doors are easy to break
- **Reinforced strike plates** with long screws into the frame
- **Door viewer or doorbell camera** so you can see who's there without opening up
- **Locks in good condition.** Sticky or loose locks get left unlocked.

If you've recently moved or lost a key, read [rekey or replace your locks](/blog/rekey-or-replace-locks/).

## Patio and sliding doors

- Use the lock every time
- Add a security bar or auxiliary lock in the track
- Check that the door can't be lifted off its track when locked
- Keep the view from outside limited with blinds at night

## The garage

The garage is a common way into a home, and it's easy to overlook.

- **Keep the garage door closed** when you're not in the garage
- **Lock the door between the garage and the house** like any exterior door
- **Don't leave the opener remote in a car parked outside**; use a keychain remote instead
- **Use a smart opener** that alerts you if the door is left open
- **Secure the emergency release** with a shield or zip-tie approach recommended by your installer to discourage fishing it open
- **Keep the door in good repair.** A door that doesn't close fully or reverses because of sensor problems gets left open. See [garage door safety sensors](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors/).
- **Cover the garage with a camera** from the driveway and, if you store valuables, inside too

## Windows

- Lock ground-floor and basement windows
- Consider window locks or pins on older sliders
- Trim shrubs that hide windows from the street

## Lighting

- Motion-activated lights at the front, back, side, and garage
- Porch lights on a timer or smart schedule
- Well-lit driveway and walkways

## Cameras

A camera system deters, alerts, and records. For most homes, cover:

1. Front door
2. Driveway and garage
3. Side gates
4. Back door and patio
5. Backyard

Wired PoE cameras with a local recorder, AI person detection, and phone alerts give the most reliable coverage. See [planning a home security camera system](/blog/home-security-camera-system-guide/) and [where to put cameras](/blog/where-to-place-security-cameras/).

## Everyday habits

- Lock doors even when you're home, including the garage entry door
- Don't hide spare keys under mats or rocks; use a lockbox or trusted neighbour
- Pause mail and deliveries when travelling, or ask a neighbour to collect them
- Don't announce trips on social media until you're back
- Reset garage door codes and lock keys after contractors, renovations, or a move
- Keep an inventory with photos of valuables for insurance

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the most common way burglars get into a house?

Opportunistic break-ins often happen through unlocked doors, open garages, or weak back and side entrances. Covering the basics makes a real difference.

### Do security cameras really deter burglars?

Visible cameras and good lighting make a property less attractive to opportunists, and recorded footage helps if something does happen.

### How can I secure my garage door?

Keep it closed, lock the interior entry door, avoid leaving remotes in cars parked outside, use a smart opener with alerts, and keep the door in good working order.

### Should I change locks after a renovation?

If contractors had keys or codes, rekeying and resetting garage codes is a sensible precaution.

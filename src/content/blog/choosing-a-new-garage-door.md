A new garage door is one of the more noticeable upgrades you can make to a home. On many houses in Durham Region, the garage door is a large part of what people see from the street. It's also a piece of equipment you'll use every day for years, so it's worth getting the details right.

This guide walks through the decisions, in the order we usually discuss them with homeowners.

## 1. Start with how you use the garage

- **Parking and storage only?** Durability and reliability come first.
- **Attached to the house or under a bedroom?** Insulation and a quiet opener matter more.
- **Workshop, gym, or hobby space?** Insulation, natural light from windows, and good weather sealing are worth prioritizing.
- **Main entrance for the family?** Consider higher-cycle springs and a smart opener.

## 2. Choose a material

### Steel

The most common choice in Ontario. Steel doors are durable, relatively low maintenance, and available in many styles and colours. Heavier-gauge steel resists dents better.

### Wood and wood-look

Real wood looks beautiful but needs regular finishing and is heavy. Many homeowners choose steel doors with a wood-grain finish or composite wood-look panels, which give a warm look with far less upkeep.

### Aluminum and glass

Full-view aluminum doors with glass panels give a modern look and let in plenty of light. They're popular on contemporary homes but offer less insulation unless you choose insulated glass.

## 3. Decide on insulation

Insulation affects comfort, noise, and how solid the door feels.

- **Uninsulated** doors are a single layer of steel. Fine for detached garages.
- **Polystyrene-insulated** doors add insulating panels inside the steel.
- **Polyurethane-insulated** doors have foam injected between steel layers, bonding them into a stiffer, quieter door with a higher insulating value.

For attached garages in our climate, an insulated door is usually worth it. Look at the door's R-value when comparing, and remember good weatherstripping matters too.

## 4. Pick a style

Common panel styles include:

- **Raised panel:** a traditional look that suits many older homes
- **Flush or flat panel:** clean and modern
- **Carriage house:** looks like swing-out barn doors but works as a regular overhead door
- **Long panel or ribbed:** horizontal lines that suit contemporary designs

Match the door to the architecture of the home. Take a photo of your house from the street and compare options against it.

## 5. Windows or no windows?

Windows bring daylight into the garage and add character. Consider privacy (frosted or tinted glass helps) and security: placing windows in the top section keeps them above eye level.

## 6. Colour

A garage door can either blend in with the siding or become a feature. Darker colours such as black and charcoal are popular on modern homes, while whites and neutrals suit traditional houses. Very dark doors absorb more sun, so ask about finishes rated for heat.

## 7. The opener and hardware

A new door is a good time to review the opener. Our [opener guide](/blog/choosing-a-garage-door-opener/) covers chain, belt, and wall-mount options. Also consider:

- **Springs matched to the new door's weight**, with higher-cycle options for busy households
- **Quality nylon rollers** for quieter operation
- **New weatherstripping** all around

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the best garage door material for Ontario winters?

Insulated steel is a popular all-round choice: durable, low maintenance, and available with good insulating values.

### Is an insulated garage door worth it?

For attached garages, garages with rooms above, or garages used as living or work space, usually yes. For a detached garage used only for parking, it's less important.

### Can a new door use my existing opener?

Sometimes, if the opener is in good condition and suited to the new door's weight and size. We check this during the quote.

### Do you remove the old door?

Yes, removal of the old door is part of a standard replacement installation.

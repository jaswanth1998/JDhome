Many homeowners lock the front door carefully but treat the garage as an afterthought. That's a problem. An attached garage often holds bikes, tools, and sports gear, and it usually has a door straight into the house. For an opportunist, an open or poorly secured garage can be an easy way in.

Here's how to close the gaps.

## 1. Keep the garage door closed

It sounds obvious, but a garage door left open, even for an hour while you're in the backyard, advertises what's inside and gives easy access. Build habits:

- Close the door as soon as you've driven in or out
- Don't leave it open while you're away from the front of the house
- Use a **smart opener** that alerts your phone when the door is left open and lets you close it remotely. See [choosing a garage door opener](/blog/choosing-a-garage-door-opener/).

## 2. Protect the opener remote

A remote left in a car parked on the driveway is a key to your house. If someone breaks into the car, they can open the garage.

- Use a **keychain remote** instead of a visor clip when parking outside
- Lock the car, and don't leave the remote visible
- Many openers have a **lock or vacation mode** on the wall control that disables remotes while you're away

## 3. Use modern rolling-code technology

Older openers used fixed codes that could be copied. Modern openers use **rolling codes** that change every time. If your opener is very old, replacing it improves security as well as convenience. After a move, reprogram remotes and change keypad codes.

## 4. Secure the emergency release

The emergency release cord lets the door be opened manually during a power outage. On some doors, it can be reached from outside through the top of the door with a tool. Ask your technician about release shields or other ways to make it harder to trip from outside while still usable from inside in an emergency.

## 5. Lock the door between the garage and the house

Treat the interior garage door like an exterior door:

- Install a **deadbolt**, not just a knob lock
- Use a solid-core or metal door with a sturdy frame
- Keep it locked, especially at night and when you're away

Our [home security checklist](/blog/home-security-checklist-durham/) and [rekey or replace your locks](/blog/rekey-or-replace-locks/) guides cover lock choices.

## 6. Cover garage windows and side doors

- Frost or cover garage windows so valuables aren't visible
- Lock side entry doors with a deadbolt
- Keep side doors and windows well lit at night

## 7. Keep the door in good working order

A garage door that doesn't close fully, reverses unexpectedly, or has to be forced gets left open or unlocked. Fix these issues promptly:

- Sensor problems: see [garage door safety sensors](/blog/garage-door-safety-sensors/)
- A heavy or unbalanced door: see [broken garage door springs](/blog/broken-garage-door-spring/)
- Worn weatherstripping that leaves gaps at the bottom

## 8. Add a camera watching the garage

Cameras are one of the most effective additions for garage security:

- An **outdoor camera** at the garage corner covers the driveway, garage door, and cars
- An **indoor camera** watches the inside of the door and the entry door to the house
- **AI person and vehicle detection** sends alerts only when someone approaches, not when leaves blow past. See [AI detection explained](/blog/ai-person-detection-cameras/).

We install wired PoE cameras with local recording and phone access through our [security camera installation service](/services/security-camera-installation/).

## 9. Light it up

Motion-activated lights over the garage door and side entry make it harder to work unseen and improve camera footage at night.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can garage doors be opened from outside with a tool?

On some doors, the emergency release can be tripped from outside. Release shields and other measures can make that much harder. Ask a technician to check your setup.

### Is it safe to leave my garage remote in my car?

It's safer to take it with you, or use a keychain remote, especially if the car is parked outside.

### Do smart garage door openers improve security?

They can, by alerting you when the door is left open and letting you close it remotely. Keep the app account secured with a strong password.

### Should I put a camera inside my garage?

If you store valuables in the garage, an interior camera watching the door and entry to the house adds useful coverage.

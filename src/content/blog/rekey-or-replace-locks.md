You just got the keys to a new home. It's exciting, but there's a question worth asking: **who else has a key?** Previous owners, their family, contractors, a dog walker, a neighbour who watered the plants. Changing who can open your doors is one of the first things security experts recommend after a move.

You have two options: **rekey** the existing locks or **replace** them. Here's how to choose.

## What rekeying means

Inside most pin-tumbler locks is a set of small pins sized to match a specific key. Rekeying means a locksmith takes the lock cylinder apart and replaces those pins with a new set that matches a new key.

- The lock hardware stays on your door
- Old keys stop working
- You get new keys
- Several locks can be rekeyed to work with **one key**

## What replacing means

Replacing means removing the lock hardware entirely and installing a new deadbolt, handle, or lockset.

- You can upgrade to stronger or better-looking hardware
- You can switch styles, such as a lever instead of a knob
- It's a good time to add a smart lock

## When rekeying makes sense

- The locks are in good working condition
- You like the look of the hardware
- You want old keys to stop working quickly
- You'd like one key for the front door, back door, and garage entry door
- You're a landlord changing tenants

## When replacing makes sense

- Locks are worn, sticky, loose, or damaged
- You want stronger security, such as a better-grade deadbolt
- The hardware doesn't match your style
- You want keyless entry or a smart lock
- A lock was damaged in a break-in attempt

## Choosing stronger hardware

If you replace, look for:

- **A proper deadbolt** on every exterior door, with a full-length bolt throw
- **Quality ratings.** Locks are often graded under ANSI/BHMA standards; higher grades are built for more demanding use.
- **Reinforced strike plates** fastened with long screws into the door frame, not just the trim
- **Matching keyways** so one key can work several doors

A lock is only as strong as the door and frame it's mounted in. Loose frames and short screws are common weak points.

## Don't forget these doors

When rekeying or replacing, think about every way into the house:

- Front and back doors
- Side doors and the door between the garage and the house
- Patio doors (often a weak point; consider an auxiliary lock or bar)
- Sheds and detached garages
- Garage door opener remotes and keypad codes: reset or reprogram them after a move

## For landlords

Rekeying between tenants is standard good practice. It's quicker and usually more economical than replacing hardware, and it ensures former tenants' keys no longer work. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is rekeying cheaper than replacing locks?

Generally yes, because the existing hardware stays in place and only the internal pins change.

### Can all my locks use the same key?

Often, yes, if they share a compatible keyway. A locksmith can rekey them to one key or advise on replacement hardware that will.

### Should I change my locks after buying a house?

It's a widely recommended step, because you can't know how many copies of the old keys exist.

### Does rekeying affect a smart lock?

Many smart locks include a physical key cylinder that can be rekeyed. You should also reset codes and remove old users in the app.

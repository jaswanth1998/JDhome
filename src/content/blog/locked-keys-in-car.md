It happens to everyone eventually: the door shuts, you hear the click, and your keys are sitting on the seat. Maybe you're in a grocery store parking lot in Whitby, at the GO station, or in your own driveway in the middle of winter. Here's what to do, step by step.

## First: is anyone or anything inside?

**If a child or pet is locked in the car, call 911 right away**, especially in hot or cold weather. Temperatures inside a closed car can become dangerous quickly. Don't wait for a locksmith.

## Check the simple things

Before calling anyone, take a minute to check:

1. **Every door and the trunk.** One door may be unlocked.
2. **The trunk release.** Some cars can be opened from the trunk, then you can reach the cabin through fold-down seats.
3. **Your phone app.** Many newer vehicles have a manufacturer app that can unlock the doors remotely.
4. **A spare key.** Is there one at home that someone could bring?
5. **Roadside assistance.** Check your auto insurance, car manufacturer coverage, or auto club membership.

## What not to do

- **Don't use a coat hanger or slim jim.** Modern car doors contain wiring, airbag components, and window mechanisms that are easily damaged.
- **Don't pry the door or window.** Bent frames and broken weatherstripping lead to leaks and wind noise.
- **Don't break a window** unless there's an emergency with a person or pet inside and help hasn't arrived.

## Preventing the next lockout

- **Keep a spare key** somewhere accessible, such as with a trusted family member
- **Set up your vehicle's app** if it supports remote unlocking
- **Build a habit:** keys in hand before the door closes
- **Replace a failing key fob battery** before it quits
- **Be extra careful in winter** when you're warming up the car; don't leave keys inside with the doors unlocked or locked

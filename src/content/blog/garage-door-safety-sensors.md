If your garage door starts to close, then stops and goes back up, the safety sensors are almost always involved. They're a small part with a big job: keeping the door from closing on a person, pet, or car.

Here's how they work, how to troubleshoot them, and the simple safety tests every household should run.

## How photo-eye sensors work

Two small sensors are mounted on either side of the door opening, a few inches above the floor. One sends an invisible infrared beam; the other receives it. When the door is closing and something breaks the beam, the opener stops and reverses.

Modern openers also have a **force-sensing reverse**: if the door meets resistance as it closes, it reverses. The sensors and the force setting work together.

## Signs of a sensor problem

- The door closes partway, then reverses
- The door only closes if you hold the wall button down
- The opener light blinks after trying to close
- One or both sensor indicator lights are off or blinking

## Troubleshooting step by step

### 1. Clear the doorway

Look for anything in the beam's path: a shovel, bin, boots, or a bike wheel. Even a spider web across the lens can do it.

### 2. Clean the lenses

Dust, dirt, and road-salt spray build up, especially in winter. Wipe both lenses gently with a soft, dry cloth.

### 3. Check the indicator lights

Most sensors have a small light on each unit. Typically, a steady light on both means they're aligned and powered. A blinking or dark light on the receiving sensor usually means misalignment. Check your opener's manual for the exact meaning.

### 4. Realign the sensors

Sensors get knocked out of position by brooms, bikes, and car doors.

1. Loosen the wing nut or screw on the sensor bracket slightly.
2. Adjust the sensor until it points directly at the other one and the light goes steady.
3. Tighten it, then test the door.

A string line or level between the two sensors helps get them at the same height.

### 5. Check the wiring

Look along the thin low-voltage wires from each sensor to the opener. Staples, chewed insulation, or a wire pinched by storage can interrupt the signal.

### 6. Shield from direct sunlight

Low morning or evening sun shining straight into the receiving sensor can blind it. If the problem happens only at certain times of day, sunlight may be the cause. Swapping the sender and receiver sides, or adding a shade, can help.

## Monthly safety tests

Do these once a month. They take two minutes.

1. **Photo-eye test:** start closing the door and wave an object (not a hand or foot) through the beam. The door should stop and reverse.
2. **Reverse test:** place a piece of wood flat on the floor in the door's path and close the door. It should reverse as soon as it contacts the wood.
3. **Balance test:** with the door closed, disconnect the opener and lift the door halfway. It should stay roughly in place.

If the door doesn't reverse, **stop using the automatic opener** until it's fixed.

> **Never bypass the sensors.** Taping them together, removing them, or holding the wall button to force the door closed defeats the safety system that protects children and pets.

- Sensors are aligned and clean but the door still reverses

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my garage door close only when I hold the button?

Many openers allow the door to close while the wall button is held down, even if the sensors are blocked. It means the sensors aren't seeing each other. Clean and align them.

### How high should garage door sensors be?

They're typically mounted a few inches above the floor, per the opener manufacturer's instructions. Both sensors should be at the same height and facing each other.

### Can snow or ice affect the sensors?

Yes. Snow buildup, ice, and salt residue can block the lens or push a sensor out of alignment. Keep the area clear in winter.

### Can sensors be replaced separately from the opener?

Usually, yes, with compatible sensors for your opener brand and model.
